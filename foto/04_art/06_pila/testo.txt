Title: Pila
Place: Barcelona, Spain, 2026
Tags:
Location: Barcelona, Spain

The lamp emerges from the layering of shaped plexiglass sheets, stacked one upon another to build a volume that is light, transparent, and dynamic. In this constructive principle lies an echo of an older, simpler gesture: that of stacking, interlocking, layering — the elemental pleasure of building by addition, with no plan to execute but a rule to discover through play. It is an open sequence of balances, a structure that accepts being remade infinitely. In this sense the lamp is, literally, without end — not because it lacks completeness, but because its nature is to begin again.

The light source remains hidden within the structure: light doesn’t appear as a direct point, but diffuses through the edges and surfaces of the material, turning each layer into an active element of the composition. The design is conceived as a modular, customizable system: each element can be oriented, rotated, arranged differently, and the user a true homo ludens becomes the author of a geometry that is never fixed. It’s a minimal gesture moving a layer, changing its angle that returns an ancient pleasure: that of manipulating matter to see what happens, of playing with light the way one plays with blocks, with no purpose beyond discovering the next form. Between hidden light, transparency, and movement, the object doesn’t simply illuminate space: it invites a playful, direct relationship between form and light one to interact with, to experiment with even just for the sake of play one of the most authentic ways of knowing matter and inhabiting its possibilities.
