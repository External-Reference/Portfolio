007

Punta del Chileno

Maldonado, Uruguay2024

ProjectPunta del Chileno
LocationMaldonado, Uruguay
TypologyResort masterplan
AuthorExternal Reference
StatusConcept

The project envisions a sustainable luxury development that harmoniously integrates contemporary architecture with Uruguay’s natural landscape. Preserving the existing pine forest, the masterplan carefully shapes circulation, plots, and buildings around the site’s topography and views. Local materials, native vegetation, organic farming, natural water filtration, and sustainable construction techniques create a refined community that balances luxury, wellbeing, and environmental stewardship.

The villas are constructed with locally sourced wood and rammed earth. Carefully positioned to complement the natural topography, each villa maximizes its connection to the surrounding landscape and views.

The design process follows a minimum-intervention approach: only the trees necessary to accommodate the main roads and building plots are removed. The remaining vegetation is arranged to optimize each villa’s views, strategically retaining shorter trees as the layout approaches the sea-view front. A grading system, carried out through spatial analysis tools, ranks each plot according to its visibility.

The natural pools are designed with a natural filtration system that significantly reduces the need for chemicals, creating a healthier and more environmentally sensitive swimming experience within the surrounding landscape.

Järvenpää

Järvenpää, Finland2009

ProjectJärvenpää
LocationJärvenpää, Finland
Area130,000 m²
AuthorExternal Reference
StatusConcept design proposal

The urban development proposal for the Tuusulanjärvi Lake area in Finland aims to harmonize natural and artificial landscapes, inspired by the interconnected structure of water and land in Finland’s Lake District. The design incorporates “eco-formations,” including water-terracing systems that channel and purify rainwater, improving the lake’s ecological health and organizing housing and services around these sustainable features.

The project seeks a zero-carbon footprint, using renewable energy sources like wind turbines and solar panels, bioclimatic buildings made from local, recyclable materials, and electric public transportation. Pedestrian and cycling paths are prioritized to minimize the use of polluting vehicles. Public spaces, recreational areas, and solutions for recycling, biomass heating, and noise management complete the design, fostering a strong connection between residents and nature.

Karakum Hotel

Ashgabat, Turkmenistan2018

ProjectKarakum Hotel
LocationAshgabat, Turkmenistan
TypologyHotel masterplan
Area68,900 m²
AuthorExternal Reference
StatusConcept design proposal

Karakum Mirage, near Ashgabat’s airport, is an architectural landmark inspired by the Karakum Desert. Its design echoes the undulating dunes and incorporates water and vegetation to create an urban oasis. Visible from all parts of the city, the project reshapes Ashgabat’s skyline.

Using computational wind analysis, the building’s three masses are separated to promote natural ventilation. Contour lines around the structures provide thermal insulation, reducing heat gain while shielding the interiors from direct sunlight. The design blends desert aesthetics with environmental performance, establishing Karakum Mirage as a symbol of the city’s growth.

Cementerio Vespella de Gaià

Vespella de Gaià, Spain2007

ProjectCementerio Vespella de Gaià
LocationVespella de Gaià, Spain
Area50,000 m²
AuthorExternal Reference
StatusConcept design proposal, awarded

Every culture develops its own responses to death, defining rituals of reverence and methods of preservation. Ancestral Etruscan practices echo in the approach: tombs were seen as the dwellings of the dead, built as cylindrical structures integrated with the landscape.

Natural light is the key element: light from above symbolizes the connection between life and the afterlife and functions as a natural clock, as in Rome’s Pantheon. The proposal combines crater-type tombs, intimate lower-level spaces where only the sky is visible, with built micropantheons resembling small tholoi or funerary amphoras for family tombs, and a cemetery church that completes a contemplative landscape.

Escalera y Mirador Vela

Barcelona, Spain2022

ProjectEscalera y Mirador Vela
ClientNova Bocana
LocationBarcelona, Spain
TypologyUrban design
Area800 m²
AuthorExternal Reference
EngineeringPIGRA
StatusBuilt

Located along Barcelona’s waterfront near the W Hotel, the project introduces a sculptural staircase and elevated walkway that connect two levels of the promenade. Inspired by Mediterranean salt crystal formations, the intervention creates a new façade and public balcony that extends the promenade toward the sea while framing views of the coastline and city.

The intervention uses the existing seawall as both support and context, turning a former dead end into an urban connection. Its prefabricated reinforced concrete structure includes a 48-step stairway and an elevated walkway, clad with triangular bio-receptive panels developed through parametric design. The panels embed native plants such as Lobularia maritima, Lampranthus spectabilis, and Drosanthemum floribundum. A fiber optic lighting system activates the façade at sunset.
