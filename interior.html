Alkimia, a Michelin-starred restaurant in Barcelona, resides in a historic modernist building from 1864, unoccupied for four decades. The renovation honors the building’s heritage while aligning with Alkimia’s innovative gastronomy.

This bar embodies three tanks in the lower part and three in the upper part that connect directly to the factory tanks located below where the beer is produced. In addition, this bar is filled with limited-edition

beers in one-liter bottles. The graphic elements and signage are modified according to the requirements. This second space is characterized by walls that exhibit the brand t-shirts, gadgets, merchandising elements, and books related to the world of beer.

The space accommodates meetings, exhibitions, and performances, with an exterior area for storage and private meetings.

At its core is Meteorite, a glass and steel structure inspired by pallasite meteors. It houses 21 transparent capsules cultivating microalgae like Chlorella Vulgaris and Synechocystis 6803, which generate oxygen and absorb CO₂—producing 10 liters of oxygen per day, 8-10 times more than a conifer forest.

Created using computational design and traditional Murano glassblowing, Meteorite was presented at HOST Milano 2017, showcasing algae’s gastronomic potential with chef Graziano Prest.

Through a sequence of immersive spaces, from historic sea routes to a deep ocean installation, the pavilion transformed nautical discoveries into a sensory experience while highlighting Spain’s commitment to ocean research and sustainability.

The exhibition unfolds in four stages, guiding visitors from cartographic routes to the depths of the ocean. The central narrative links Columbus, Malaspina, and the Malaspina 2010 expedition, showing how exploration evolved from navigation to scientific research. In the Deep Ocean space, water samples collected at 5,000 meters become the

For the 2023 Guadalajara International Book Fair, the European Union Pavilion was designed under the theme “Building a Union of Cultures.” Inspired by the New European Bauhaus, it combines geometric forms, cultural spaces, and sustainable materials to create an experiential environment that strengthens ties between the EU, Mexico, and Latin America.

The pavilion brings together a bookstore, central agora, digital media library, and interactive installation within a cohesive spatial system. Colour and geometry help spatially define each function.
